# New talent orientation — working under Future Modern

**For:** talent joining FM who haven't worked in a managed
engagement before (independent producers, self-taught devs, solo
freelancers).
**Ships as:** a required read inside the care-package onboarding
flow, with an "I understand" acknowledgment tracked in
`onboarding_completion`.
**Purpose:** name the norms before your first client call, not
after.

---

## The one-sentence version

Future Modern is the contract of record. That means the client hired
FM. FM hired you. On every client-facing surface, act like that's
the arrangement — because it is.

## The five things that matter most

### 1. Every client communication routes through FM.

Email, chat, calls, invoices, timesheets, quotes, status updates —
all of it. CC the FM account owner on every message to the client,
or route through them first. If you're not sure whether something
should go to the client, don't — ask the account owner first.

**Why:** the client's relationship is with FM. When you send them
something directly, you skip the person whose job is to manage that
relationship. Even a small thing can undercut the account owner in
ways you won't see, and if it goes sideways later, FM is the one
holding the bag.

### 2. Your rate is locked at engagement acceptance.

The rate agreed in your LOI is the rate you're paid for the
duration of that engagement. You don't quote a different rate to
the client. You don't invoice a different rate to the client.

FM's standard practice: talent give a rate (or range), FM quotes
the client at the high end of that range, pays the talent at the
low end as a baseline, and distributes performance bonuses on top.
That's how FM protects margin *and* rewards good work. If your
range was wrong, tell the account owner — but the number in the
LOI is the number for the engagement.

**Scope changes are different.** If the client asks for
substantially more than what was scoped, that's a scope amendment,
and rate/timeline get renegotiated as part of the amendment. This
is standard, especially on fixed-price engagements. Scope
amendments happen agency-to-client through the account owner —
never contractor-to-client on a side channel.

### 3. FM sets commercial terms.

Payment schedule, deposits, late-payment interest, kill fees,
retainers, credit terms — FM sets these with the client. You don't.
Even if you have strong opinions about how the arrangement should
work, don't propose it to the client. Bring it to the account
owner. FM will decide whether to change the arrangement.

**Why:** commercial terms are how FM manages risk across the whole
portfolio, not just your engagement. A contractor proposing
deposit-on-signature to a government client, for example, misreads
how public-sector payment cycles work and can kill a relationship
FM took years to build.

### 4. FM is the contract of record, not a broker.

If a client casually asks "so are you guys just brokers?" the
answer is no. FM is the delivery entity. FM signed the SOW. FM is
responsible for the outcome. You're a Future Modern engagement —
that's how the work is described to the client, in every
conversation.

**Why this matters:** clients who think of FM as "just a broker"
start routing around FM to work with contractors directly next
time. That kills FM's pipeline and, over time, kills the pipeline
that brought you the engagement in the first place. Talent who
minimize FM's role in front of clients don't get renewed.

### 5. Payment is downstream of client payment.

FM pays you within the number of days named in your LOI *after*
FM receives payment from the client. If the client is on 60-day
terms, or on appropriations (common for government), payment
takes as long as it takes. FM doesn't sit on funds. Asking the
account owner for a status update is fine. Escalating to the
client, or applying pressure to FM as if FM is the problem, is
not fine.

**Why:** if you take FM engagements, you're taking on the payment
cycle of the client type FM works with. That risk is priced into
your rate. If you can't take that risk, you should know that
before you accept.

## The rest of the norms, briefly

- **Client info is confidential.** Don't share client name,
  engagement economics, or deliverables outside FM without written
  approval.
- **Your rate and FM's markup are confidential *to the client*.**
  Don't disclose your rate or FM's markup to the client. Talent are
  free to talk about rates with each other — FM is labor-first, and
  cross-contractor transparency is welcome. FM will also be honest
  with you about your rate relative to the quality of your
  portfolio and skillset.
- **Portfolio references go through review.** After delivery, submit
  work to `/profile/portfolio`. Admin reviews and publishes with any
  necessary scrubs before it goes public.
- **Non-solicit for 24 months.** You can't take an FM client for
  outside work for two years after the engagement ends. Contract-
  to-hire engagements carry a 12-month non-solicit instead, since
  direct handoff is the point. If a client reaches out to you
  unsolicited, tell the account owner right away — that's not a
  violation as long as you route it back through FM.
- **Direct feedback is welcome.** If you disagree with how FM is
  handling something, bring it up with the account owner directly.
  Don't work it out with the client.

## What happens if you cross a line

FM tracks conduct on the platform through two layers. Neither is
punitive by default — both exist to keep the standard clear and
consistent for everyone.

- **Peer reviews** include a professionalism rating from your
  teammates on multi-person engagements. Soft, sentiment layer.
- **Compliance penalties** are admin-adjudicated for the specific
  Conduct Standards violations named in your LOI. Each penalty is
  -9 OVR for 90 days and rolls off automatically. If a pattern
  doesn't correct, penalties stack.

The first time something happens, expect a direct conversation
with the account owner. The line will be named specifically. If it
happens again, the engagement can end. If it's egregious, the
engagement can end the first time.

## Why the standard is set this way

FM's business is trust — clients trust that if they hire FM,
they'll get delivery from someone who represents FM well and won't
create side-channel messes. Talent trust that if they take an FM
engagement, they'll get paid and their reputation will be
protected. Both sides only work if the standard is enforced
consistently.

None of this is about micromanaging you. It's about naming the
minimum bar so nobody has to guess. Deliver good work, keep client
comms clean, don't renegotiate rate after acceptance, and represent
FM well — that covers 95% of it. The rest is judgment, and the
account owner is a phone call away when you're not sure.

Welcome in.
